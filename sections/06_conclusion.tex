\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper derived a dynamic discharge tariff for the Høje Taastrup PTES from the marginal values of a coordinated heat-system schedule and evaluated it against the measured operation in 2024. Under the current settlement, implementing scheduled discharge does not reduce the local operator's heat payment and adds pumping cost. A discharge credit equal to the spread between the value of delivered and of stored heat aligns the local decision with the system value.

Three conclusions follow. First, a small credit is sufficient: a bounded hourly credit with a mean of 5~DKK/MWh doubles PTES discharge, recovers 79\% of the coordination value of 4.86~MDKK per year and leaves both the local operator and the upstream side better off in almost all weeks. Second, timing matters: the hourly credit saves 0.47~MDKK per year more than a constant credit of the same mean, because it rewards discharge only when the system benefits. Third, the share of the storage value passed to the local operator determines the distribution more than the total benefit; compared with the bounded credit, passing the full value increases welfare only slightly but transfers about four times as much to the local operator. The remaining gap is associated with local heat production, which would require a separate price signal.

Further work should use the planned 2024 schedules to assess deviations from the schedule and possible deviation charges, include measured local demand and heat-pump operation, extend the analysis to an annual horizon, and clarify how cost savings are passed on through the heat purchase agreements.
